Tab Guard

Keeps Firefox tabs from eating your Mac's memory. Free and open source.

Download for Mac macOS 13 or later · Apple silicon and Intel · signed and notarized

One runaway tab can push Firefox past 10 GB and send your Mac into swap. macOS has no way to cap an app's memory, so Tab Guard watches instead: it sits in the menu bar, checks Firefox's tab processes, and, if you let it, closes the one that grew past your limit. Firefox shows that tab as crashed, with a Restore button that brings it back.

How it works

Watch first

It starts in Watch only: it shows what it would close and why, and closes nothing until you pick a mode.

Your limits

A per-tab limit (2 GB by default) and a whole-browser limit (8 GB) that prefers idle tabs and never picks small ones.

Knows when to stop

If closing a tab doesn't bring Firefox down, it backs off, and it never closes more than a few tabs an hour.

Stays out of the way

About 16 MB of memory and no noticeable CPU. Turn it off or change modes from the menu bar.

Privacy

Everything happens on your Mac. Tab Guard never sees which website runs in which tab. It can share anonymous usage counts, such as which rule closed a tab, but only if you tick the box; it's off otherwise. The privacy notes list everything that's sent.

Install

Download the .dmg, open it, and drag Tab Guard to Applications. It updates itself after that. Prefer the source? It's on GitHub under the MIT license.
