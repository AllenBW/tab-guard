Tab Guard

Stops runaway browser tabs from eating your Mac's memory. Free and open source.

Download for Mac macOS 13 or later · Apple silicon and Intel · signed and notarized

It starts in Watch only and closes nothing until you choose. A closed tab loses anything unsaved in it.

A badly behaved website shouldn't be able to run amok with your computer. One runaway tab can grow to gigabytes and send your Mac into swap, and finding and closing it by hand is tedious. macOS has no way to cap an app's memory, so Tab Guard watches instead: it sits in the menu bar, checks your browser's tab processes, and, if you let it, closes the one that grew past your limit. The browser shows that tab as crashed, with a button that reloads the page. Anything unsaved in it is lost.

Works with Firefox and browsers built on it (Zen, LibreWolf, Waterfox, Floorp and more) and with Chrome and its relatives (Edge, Brave, Arc, Vivaldi, Opera). Not Safari: its page processes are shared with Mail and other apps, so there's no telling which are its tabs.

How it works

Watch first

It starts in Watch only: it shows what it would close and why, and closes nothing until you pick a mode.

Your limits

A per-tab limit (2 GB by default) and a whole-browser limit (8 GB) that closes the biggest tab, preferring an idle one, and never one under 400 MB. Each browser you run gets its own limits.

Knows when to stop

One close at a time, at least a minute apart. The whole-browser limit closes at most 3 tabs an hour, and pauses for 15 minutes if a close doesn't lower the browser's total.

Stays out of the way

About 19 MB of memory with its window closed, measured on an Apple silicon Mac. Pause it from the menu bar for an hour, until morning, or until you turn it back on.

Before you turn on closing

Closing a tab is abrupt: it just shows as crashed, and anything unsaved in it is gone. Keep Tab Guard in Watch only, or pause it from the menu bar for an hour (it turns itself back on), while you're on a call, presenting, or doing work you can't lose. One process can hold several tabs from the same site, so closing it crashes all of them. It judges by memory alone, and can't tell which website a process is. The README lists the rest.

Privacy

Tab Guard runs on your Mac and never sees which website runs in which tab. It checks GitHub for a new version every 6 hours and installs it; you can turn that off in Limits & settings. It can share anonymous usage counts, such as which rule closed a tab, but only if you tick the box; it's off otherwise. The privacy notes list everything that's sent.

Install

Download the .dmg, open it, and drag Tab Guard to Applications. It updates itself after that. The source is on GitHub under the MIT license.
